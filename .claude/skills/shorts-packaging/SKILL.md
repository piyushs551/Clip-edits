# Shorts packaging

Adapted from the MIT-licensed bundle https://github.com/sergebulaev/youtube-skills (hook, title, description and planner skills), cut down for text-only short-form video. No accounts, API keys or scripts are needed or wanted.

## Core idea
A short video earns the view in two places: the packaging (title or caption, plus the first frame) earns the click or the stop, and the first 3 seconds plus the ending earn the watch time. Optimize both, and never promise more than the video delivers.

## Defaults for this user
- Channel: DailyClip. Gaming first (GTA 6), but keep wording usable for other topics later.
- Text-only: no voiceover and no speaking on camera. All hooks are on-screen text that works with the sound off.
- Never invent a number, date or result. Use only what the user said or what is visible in the footage. If a detail is missing, ask once; if there is still nothing, leave the claim out.
- Keep story games spoiler-free. Do not use trailer footage or the game's soundtrack.

## 1. Hook (first 3 seconds)
- Frame one states the payoff or the tension. No greeting, no logo, no slow intro.
- On-screen text, 6 words or fewer per line, bold white with a dark outline, mid-screen.
- Good shapes: a concrete fact ("No disc. Just a code."), a countdown ("4 days away"), a surprise from the footage, a plain question.
- Design the loop: the last line or shot should flow back into the first line so the video replays well.

## 2. On-screen text script
Write a table: time window, shot, text. Keep each line to 6 words or fewer, one idea per line, and change something on screen every 2 to 4 seconds. End with a short call to action such as "Follow for a clip every day."

## 3. Titles and captions
Write 3 to 5 variants and mark the one to use.
- Front-load the click-deciding words. Aim for 40 to 60 characters; hard cap 100 on YouTube.
- Use one concrete noun and, where true, one specific number.
- Honest curiosity only. No "you won't believe", no ALL CAPS, at most 1 emoji.
- Do not repeat the on-screen hook word for word; the title adds context the video text does not.
- Useful shapes: curiosity gap, number, transformation ("I did X for N days"), versus.

## 4. Per-platform output
- **YouTube Short (regular app):** title field plus description. Title can carry 2 to 4 hashtags. Description: first 150 characters work as a second hook, then one line on the series, then 3 to 5 hashtags. Audience must be set to "not made for kids".
- **TikTok:** one caption, 1 to 2 short sentences, a question to invite comments, 4 to 5 hashtags. Choose a sound from TikTok's library at low volume. Comments, Duet and Stitch on; AI-generated label off for real footage.
- **Instagram Reel:** caption with a question and one line of series branding, 3 to 5 hashtags. Share to Facebook OFF to keep the account separate from the personal one. Add alt text.
- Sound: suggest search terms inside each app, never a specific copyrighted song. For a retro Miami or Vice City feel: synthwave, retrowave, 80s Miami. Volume 10 to 20 percent.
- First comment on each post: a question that is easy to answer.

## 5. Planning
- Cadence must be one the user can keep for 12 weeks. Daily Shorts are fine if each takes under 30 minutes.
- Vary the pillar across the week (news or countdown, tips, reaction, gameplay moment). Do not run five copies of one idea.
- Shorts should point at something bigger over time (a longer video, a series). Track Shorts views separately from returning viewers.
- After a post has a day of data, ask the user for views and retention on each app, then say which format to repeat.

## Checks before handing over
- Does the title promise only what the first 30 seconds show?
- Is every number or date from the user or the footage?
- Any date-relative wording ("4 days away") correct for the planned posting day?
- Hashtags between 3 and 5 per platform?
